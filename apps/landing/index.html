Paid API calls for AI agents, with refunds

Your AI agent paid for bad data. Certo gets the money back.

An agent's payment is final. Even when the answer is wrong.

Today: the money is gone.

Your agent asks for the top holders of NEAR, pays 0.40 USDC, and gets the holders of NEO. There is no chargeback for agents.

Escrow: everyone waits.

Holding each payment until the buyer is satisfied protects the buyer, and makes every honest seller wait for its money on every call.

Certo: pay now, refund if it's bad.

The seller is paid in the same transaction. The refund risk sits on the seller's own bond, and only for 120 seconds.

Guards try to stop a bad payment before it happens. Certo gets the money back after one goes through.

Three steps, one smart contract

  1. 1The seller locks a bond.

    An API seller deposits USDC into the Certo contract on NEAR. Anyone can read the bond before paying, and a seller can never owe more in refunds than its bond holds.

  2. 2Your agent pays. The seller has the money at once.

    One transaction moves the price from the agent to the seller, minus a 1% fee. Nothing is held back.

  3. 3For 120 seconds, a bad answer can be refunded.

    If the answer is an error, missing, broken or wrong, the agent claims a refund and the contract pays it from the seller's bond. If nobody claims, the payment is final.

Why use Certo

For agent builders (buyers)

  • Try an unknown paid API without betting the money on it. A bad answer comes back out of the seller's bond.
  • One tool call gets the refund. Your agent calls certo_dispute: no support ticket, no chargeback form.
  • See the risk before you pay. certo_quote shows the seller's bond, its lost refund claims and its fee. Your agent never pays more than its cap per call (default 1.00 USDC).
  • No platform to trust. Every refund decision can be checked on chain.

For API sellers

  • Paid instantly. The money arrives in the buyer's own transaction. No escrow wait.
  • A reason to try you. A bonded badge gives cautious agent buyers a reason to call an API they would otherwise skip.
  • Your risk is capped. Open refunds can never exceed your bond.
  • A clean record lowers your fee. 1.0% at the start, down to 0.5%. Buyers who dispute honest answers lose their deposit.

Simple failures are refunded by rule. Wrong answers go to a panel.

Refunded by rule

on for every seller
  • The server returned an error (HTTP 5xx)
  • Nothing was delivered
  • The answer is broken: not JSON, or a required field is missing
  • The data is stale

Checked by the contract. No judgement and no AI involved.

Refunded by an AI panel

experimental · the seller opts in

Some answers look fine and are still wrong. "NEO" instead of "NEAR" arrives with status 200, valid JSON and every field present. No rule can catch it.

For these, three AI models from three companies (Qwen, GLM and DeepSeek) each read the request and the answer inside a secure enclave on NEAR AI Cloud, and each signs its vote. The contract checks all three signatures itself before any money moves.

How good is the panel? In our own tests it ruled correctly on 19 of 20 labelled cases, and failed 1 of 10 prompt-injection attempts. That is why it is opt-in and marked experimental. Read the test →

What stops a buyer from lying? Claiming that an answer is wrong costs the buyer a small deposit (0.02 USDC on a 0.40 call). If the experimental panel disagrees, the deposit is gone, and the next one doubles. Known gap: a buyer who never asks the panel and lets the dispute time out gets only the seller's offer, but also the deposit back, so today the deposit deters only buyers who go through the panel. Try it in three tool calls

Certo works with Claude Code, Codex and any MCP client. The playground runs on a local NEAR sandbox: no keys, no accounts.

For agent builders

npm ci && make playground
claude mcp add certo -- npx certo-mcp --playground
ToolWhat it does
certo_quoteLook before paying: the price, the seller's bond and its record.
certo_callPay and get the answer.
certo_disputeWrong answer? One call, and the money is back.

Open the quickstart ↗ llms.txt for agents

For API sellers

Get paid in the buyer's own transaction. No escrow period. Your risk is capped by the bond you choose.

npx certo seller init --bond 50 --terms terms.txt

One command registers you, publishes your terms and locks your bond. Then wrap your route with protect(). Add --panel to opt in to the experimental AI panel.

Testnet only today. No third-party seller has used Certo yet.

What is real, and what is not yet

We would rather you read this here than find it out later.

Real today

  • The contract is live on NEAR testnet (v1.certo.testnet), and its code is locked: the account has no keys left, so nobody can replace it
  • Sellers are paid in the paying transaction
  • Refunds by rule for errors, non-delivery, broken and stale data
  • Three enclave signatures of the experimental AI panel checked inside the contract
  • MCP server, CLI, buyer SDK, seller middleware
  • Ten attacks on our own contract, each refused (make guards)
  • 219 TypeScript tests, 63 Rust tests, MIT licence

Not yet

  • Test USDC only. No mainnet, no real value
  • Both demo sellers are ours. No third party has used it
  • The AI panel is experimental: it failed 1 of 10 prompt-injection tests
  • The replay's AI answers are recorded, not asked live
  • Enclave certificate chains (Intel, NVIDIA) are not verified
  • The contract owner can replace the panel's keys. It is visible on chain; there is no timelock
  • A stock x402 client cannot pay a Certo seller yet

Where this goes

Not built yet

Certo is the refund layer: the aim is to make agent payments refundable on any rail, not to replace the rails.

Next

  • Spending limits the owner sets for their agent: a daily budget, a minimum seller bond
  • Statements for the owner: spent, refunded and net, per seller
  • An AI panel (experimental today) hardened to pass its injection test

Then

  • Refund cover for any x402 payment, not only payments through Certo
  • Metered and streaming calls
  • Other currencies through NEAR Intents

Later

  • Third parties posting bonds for sellers (underwriting)
  • Refunds that pass back down a chain of agents reselling to each other
  • Mainnet with real USDC and time-locked governance
